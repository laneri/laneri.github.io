# Abstract

We numerically investigate the geometry and transport properties of infection fronts within the spatial SIR
model in two dimensions. The model incorporates short-range correlated quenched random transmission rates.
Our findings reveal that the critical average transmission rate for the steady-state propagation of the infection is
overestimated by the naive mean-field homogenization. Furthermore, we observe that the velocity, profile, and
harmfulness of the fronts, given a specific average transmission, are sensitive to the details of randomness.
In particular, we find that the harmfulness of the front is larger the more uniform the transmission rate is,
suggesting potential optimization in vaccination strategies under constraints like fixed average-transmission rates
or limited vaccine resources. The large-scale geometry of the advancing fronts presents nevertheless robust
universal features and, for a statistically isotropic and short-range correlated disorder, we get a roughness
exponent α ≈ 0.42 ± 0.10 and a dynamical exponent z ≈ 1.6 ± 0.10, which are roughly compatible with the
one-dimensional Kardar-Parisi-Zhang (KPZ) universality class. We find that the KPZ term and the disorder-
induced effective noise are present and have a kinematic origin.
